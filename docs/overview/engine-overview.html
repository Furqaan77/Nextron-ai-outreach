Nextron AI · internal build · architecture overview

Nextron Outreach Engine

A program on one office PC that finds companies with a visible reason to need Nextron, writes short emails in Furqaan's name that cite that reason, and sends nothing until a person approves it. Interested replies reach Furqaan's phone within minutes.

US + UK first Law firms · Ops-heavy mid-size companies Every email human-approved ≈ $70–85 / month at full volume
How a lead moves through it

From a stranger to a booked call

Each step saves its work to the database on the PC before the next step starts, so sleep, a reboot or a lost connection never loses anything. The one step the engine can't do alone is approval.

What keeps it safe

Rules built into the design

One door for every email

Only one part of the program can send. At the last second it re-checks the unsubscribe list, office hours, daily limits and the domain's email authentication. Each email is written down before it goes, so a crash can't cause a duplicate.

Approval is tied to the exact words

What you approved is what goes out. Change a word and it needs approving again. Approvals older than 3 days return to the queue.

No invented facts

Emails may only mention things found on a real page (with its link saved) or listed in Nextron's Approved Company Facts. A checker blocks anything else before it reaches you.

Within the law

US and UK only. In the UK, only limited companies and LLPs. One-click unsubscribe in every email, a postal address in the footer, and opt-outs honored immediately and forever. An opt-out blocks every channel: email, LinkedIn notes and call sheets.

"Remove me" wins instantly

Any human reply pauses that person's emails the moment it arrives. Opt-out wording is caught by fixed rules, so the AI's reading of a reply can never undo it.

Test mode can't reach prospects

Any test copy of the engine has its own database and can only email addresses on an approved list.

The dashboard is private

It only answers this PC, and it ignores requests from other websites, so a malicious page can't click "approve" for you.

What runs where

Almost everything stays on the PC

On the office PC

  • The engine itself. It starts automatically at logon and catches up after sleep.
  • The database: leads, evidence, drafts, sent mail and replies.
  • Passwords and access keys, locked in Windows Credential Manager.
  • Nightly backups, plus an encrypted copy kept somewhere else.

Online services it talks to

  • Gmail (sending inboxes on a separate domain, never nextronai.com)
  • Claude: Haiku reads websites, Opus writes every email and reply
  • Google Places, Companies House, Hunter for finding companies and emails
  • Telegram for Furqaan's alerts
  • A tiny free Cloudflare page that records unsubscribe clicks. It never sees names or emails.

Build order

Three releases, first emails as soon as the inboxes are warm

Release 1 · First Send

Hand-picked leads, end to end

Import a list or paste a LinkedIn post. The engine researches, drafts, queues for approval, sends, catches replies and alerts Furqaan, with every compliance rule on. Ready by the time the inboxes finish 14 days of warm-up.

Release 2 · Automated supply

The engine finds its own leads

Automatic discovery, scoring and tiers, monthly re-checks for new signals, call prep sheets and call outcome tracking.

Release 3 · Assist and insight

LinkedIn list and full analytics

The daily LinkedIn to-do list, the full funnel dashboard, settings screens and pipeline polish.
